# III.09 "Marine Water Quality and Eutrophication Indicator"

### Brief Description
The water quality index (WQI) is a method that integrates multiple water quality monitoring data into a single indicator. This indicator is calculated based on the eutrophication index (EI) developed from the assessments of coastal eutrophication. ### Background
The water quality index (WQI) is a method that integrates multiple water quality monitoring data into a single indicator. WQI has been developed for 40 years. The first widely adopted general-purpose index was proposed by Brown et al. (1970), who select 9 parameters from an initial set of 35 through expert surveys and assigned weights (Wi) based on their relative importance. This became the National Sanitation Foundation Water Quality Index (NSF-WQI) in the United States. <br> The nine parameters used in the NSF-WQI are dissolved oxygen, fecal coliform, pH, biochemical oxygen demand (BOD), nitrate nitrogen, total phosphorus, temperature, turbidity, and total solids (TS).
